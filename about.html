About

Taking the long view

Some ideas are worth carrying with you for a lifetime. The Long View shares one each evening: a calm, appreciative five minutes with a thinker who helps us see how people live together, and what keeps a civilisation kind, strong and whole.

Where it begins

The series is anchored on the American essayist Lee Harris (Civilization and Its Enemies, The Suicide of Reason), who wrote with unusual freshness about what holds societies together: shared stories, trust, belonging and the habits of cooperation. From him it widens out to the thinkers who shaped those ideas or shared their questions, from Thucydides and Ibn Khaldun to Tocqueville, Arendt and Popper.

Every thinker is presented generously, at their best: what is insightful, original and still useful. This is a place for wonder and learning rather than argument.

The rhythm of the week

  • 📜 MondayAn essay by Lee Harris
  • 🏛️ TuesdayA classic
  • 📚 WednesdayA modern thinker
  • 💡 ThursdayOne idea, explained
  • 🔍 FridayAnother angle
  • ☕ SaturdaySomething lighter
  • 🗓️ SundayThe week in one page

How each edition is made

  • A Hermes AI agent chooses one reading, fetches the page and reads it in full.
  • It writes the idea in brief from that text alone, then the bigger picture, another angle and a question to take into the evening.
  • Before anything is published, a second AI model running at home checks the summary against the source text and sends back anything the source does not support.
  • Each edition is read aloud by a text-to-speech voice (Kokoro), and paired with a freely licensed image from Wikimedia Commons.

Every edition links to its original reading, which is always worth your time. If you spot something wrong, the source is the place to check.
